1. Product Definition

What ResearchLens is

ResearchLens is an academic research decision assistant. It is designed to help students and supervisors evaluate whether a thesis idea is worth pursuing, how it should be narrowed, what literature already exists, and how the idea can be defended during topic approval.

The product sits between a search engine, a literature review assistant, and a supervisor preparation tool. Search engines return papers. General AI tools produce broad advice. ResearchLens connects the two: it retrieves evidence, scores the idea, identifies risks, and produces a practical academic decision packet.

What it does

  • Retrieves related papers from scholarly metadata sources.
  • Ranks papers by relevance to the user idea.
  • Detects overlap and novelty risk.
  • Builds a gap matrix across topic, method, population, and geography.
  • Suggests pivot directions when the idea is too crowded or vague.
  • Generates supervisor-ready notes and approval packets.
  • Supports publication, funding, and faculty-fit decisions.

What it is not

  • It is not a proof of global novelty.
  • It is not a replacement for reading papers.
  • It does not bypass paywalls or scrape publisher sites.
  • It does not guarantee supervisor approval.
  • It does not invent citations, journals, grants, or deadlines.

2. Problem

The problem ResearchLens solves

Many students begin thesis work with ideas that are interesting but not yet research-ready. They may not know whether the topic is already saturated, whether the method is feasible, whether enough evidence exists, or how to explain the novelty to a supervisor.

Student pain

Students waste weeks searching randomly, copying broad topics, and presenting vague proposals without knowing what evidence supports or threatens the idea.

Supervisor pain

Supervisors receive underdeveloped topics and must repeatedly ask for clearer scope, stronger literature, and more feasible methods.

Institution pain

Departments lose time to weak proposal cycles, inconsistent guidance, and low-quality literature preparation.

Core insight

The hard part is not only finding papers. The hard part is converting papers into a decision: proceed, narrow, redesign, broaden search, read specific papers, or explore a better pivot. ResearchLens is built around that decision layer.

3. System Workflow

How ResearchLens works

ResearchLens combines deterministic retrieval, ranking, scoring, and structured synthesis. The product is designed so that important scores and evidence boundaries are computed by code, while language models are used for explanation and packaging.

1

User submits a research idea

The user enters a thesis idea, field, academic level, language, mode, and evidence scope. The system treats the input as a research question candidate, not as a final topic.

2

Query expansion and faceted retrieval

ResearchLens expands the idea into multiple search queries and detects facets such as domain, problem, method, language context, intervention, and geography. Retrieval is then performed across supported scholarly metadata sources.

3

Deduplication and ranking

Papers are normalized by DOI, title, source identifiers, and metadata. The system ranks papers by relevance and records why each paper appears, including matched and missing facets.

4

Gap and sanity scoring

The system computes a gap matrix and Research Sanity Matrix. It scores novelty potential, evidence strength, feasibility, supervisor fit, claim risk, and overall readiness.

5

Decision packaging

The result is turned into a Decision Hub, approval packet, supervisor note, pivots, research mindmap, evidence stream, publication fit, and funding fit.

4. Technical Architecture

What ResearchLens uses

Application stack

  • Next.js App Router for frontend and API routes.
  • React and TypeScript for typed UI and domain logic.
  • Tailwind CSS for interface styling.
  • Session and browser storage for v1 report state.
  • Optional Supabase support for faculty directory persistence.

AI and retrieval

  • Gemini or Vertex AI for synthesis, copilot, query support, and summaries.
  • OpenAlex for broad scholarly metadata and venue/source enrichment.
  • Semantic Scholar for paper metadata and citation context.
  • DataCite, arXiv, CORE, Europe PMC, PubMed, Crossref, and Unpaywall paths where configured.
  • DOAJ and OpenAlex source metadata for publication targeting verification.

5. Product Modules

Main features

Decision Hub

The default results view. It shows verdict, novelty, evidence, risk, next best action, and the approval packet.

Approval Packet

A copyable supervisor-facing document with topic direction, evidence, risks, questions, and next steps.

Research Sanity Matrix

A deterministic quality-control layer that evaluates novelty potential, evidence strength, feasibility, supervisor fit, and claim risk.

Evidence Stream

Inspectable ranked papers with access labels, source metadata, DOI signals, and relevance context.

Thesis Mindmap

A visual workspace that decomposes the idea into topic, method, population, geography, evidence, risks, and pivots.

Paper Lineage

A Connected Papers-style graph for prior works, derivative works, closest neighbors, novelty threats, and method sources.

Research Fit

A publication and funding readiness surface with journal targets, route recommendations, fundability score, aims, and grant abstract.

Research Copilot

A workspace assistant that answers questions using selected card context, thesis idea, notes, papers, sanity matrix, and evidence boundaries.

8. Defensibility

Why ResearchLens works

It is evidence-first

The product starts with retrieved scholarly metadata and ranked papers before generating conclusions. This reduces generic advice and forces outputs to be grounded in actual literature signals.

It is decision-focused

The app does not stop at search. It turns evidence into verdicts, blockers, next actions, supervisor questions, and approval-ready text.

It uses deterministic guardrails

Novelty, evidence, risk, fit, and retrieval diagnostics are computed by local scoring logic. LLM synthesis is used to explain and package, not to silently decide everything.

It supports recursive refinement

Pivots, mindmaps, and lineage views help users move from a broad idea to a sharper contribution without starting from zero each time.

10. Innovation

What kind of innovation ResearchLens represents

ResearchLens is not primarily a new paper search engine. Its innovation is in the workflow layer around academic decision-making. It combines retrieval, quality scoring, visual reasoning, full-text boundaries, publication fit, funding fit, and supervisor communication into one coherent thesis approval flow.

Workflow innovation

The system turns a research idea into a sequence of academic decisions instead of a flat list of search results.

Evaluation innovation

The Research Sanity Matrix gives students a structured way to reason about novelty, evidence, feasibility, and claim risk.

Pedagogical innovation

The product teaches students how to think like reviewers and supervisors by showing what evidence supports or weakens their idea.

Product innovation

It connects thesis approval, literature discovery, supervisor communication, publication targeting, and funding preparation in one system.

12. Responsible Use

Trust, safety, and academic integrity

ResearchLens should be positioned as a research preparation and decision-support tool, not as an automated thesis writer. Its strongest long-term value depends on clear evidence boundaries, citation honesty, and responsible AI use.

Evidence boundaries

Claims should be labeled as full-text verified, abstract-supported, metadata-only, or requiring manual reading.

No fake certainty

The product should never claim complete novelty or complete literature coverage. It should explain limitations clearly.

No paywall bypass

Full-text access should rely on legal open-access links, official APIs, or user-supplied PDFs.

Student ownership

The tool should help students understand and defend their topic, not replace their reading, thinking, or writing responsibility.
